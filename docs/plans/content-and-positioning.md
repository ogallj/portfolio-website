# Content and positioning plan

Prepared 29 October 2026. Scope: information architecture, content model and professional draft copy. No UI implementation or live-site changes are included.

## Basis and writing approach

**Diagnosis: blank brief with concrete owner facts.** The owner has supplied professional roles, brands and a clear separation between software and creative/marketing work. Use those facts to establish the argument. Specific campaign responsibilities, dates, outcomes, subbrands and assets have not yet been supplied, so the draft does not invent them.

The owner has authorized an original immersive concept and wants to use free resources. Real campaign assets may remain clearly identified placeholders during development. This does not authorize presenting invented campaigns or speculative results as completed brand work.

The installed `copywriting` skill was applied: factual claims must be supportable, headings should be clear, and missing proof should become a content task. Older local website copy is useful as an inventory, but the owner's latest facts take precedence. No product-marketing context file was found in the usual local locations.

## Recommended category and positioning

Use **Digital Creative & Marketing** as the full name of the creative practice. It covers the supplied digital-creative and digital-marketing roles without limiting the collection to graphic design or implying an unsupported specialization such as paid acquisition, brand strategy or creative direction.

Use **Software Engineering** for the software practice. The stated professional role is **Frontend Software Engineer**; keep that precision when describing experience. Do not promote it to full-stack engineer, lead engineer or software architect without project evidence.

The portfolio represents **Joshua Odero Ogallo**, with **Joshua Ogallo** available as the compact display name. The full name is established in the current local site. The two practices receive separate work collections, landing pages and case studies. They should not be combined in one mixed grid or hidden behind a filter.

| Purpose | Recommended label |

“Creative Developer” would imply that experimental website development is the primary professional identity. The new website can demonstrate that ability, but the supplied career facts do not require replacing the owner's established roles with it. “Digital Creative & Marketing” remains the category recommendation even if the visual concept is highly experimental.

| `/creative-marketing/[project]/` | Explain the brief, personal contribution, deliverables and credits | View the work or contact Joshua |
| `/about/` | Provide the full professional story with separate experience groups | View CV or contact Joshua |

Keep direct navigation available during the immersive opening. The visitor should not have to finish a scene or replay an introduction to access either practice. Both client and recruiter paths matter: project pages provide proof; About and CV provide career context; Contact accepts either a project or an employment opportunity.

The software page should feature software projects, technical contribution and engineering experience. The creative page should feature campaign/creative work, brand experience and creative/marketing experience. A brand may appear in both practices only when there is a distinct, supportable contribution in each. A shared brand name does not make a software screenshot into a marketing case study.

## Case-study content model

Separate the two collections at the data/content level as well as in navigation. They may share presentation components, but each case study has one primary practice and its own route.

### Shared case-study fields

| Field | Purpose / publication rule |
|---|---|
| `id` and `slug` | Stable unique record and route identifier |
| `practice` | Exactly one of Software Engineering or Digital Creative & Marketing |
| `title` | Actual project/campaign name; a brand name alone is not automatically the project title |
| `brandDisplayName` | The owner-supplied or later confirmed brand name |
| `summary` | One specific sentence about the work delivered, supported by project facts |
| `role` | Joshua's actual role for this project; do not inherit a grander role from old copy |
| `contribution` | What Joshua personally did, distinct from team output |
| `deliveryContext` | Direct, through agency, through employer, freelance platform, or unresolved |
| `agencyOrEmployer` | Named only when its relationship to this project is established |
| `teamAndCredits` | Collaborators and responsibilities, where known |
| `dateOrPeriod` | Omit publicly until confirmed; do not infer from file timestamps |
| `brief` | The actual problem or request |
| `deliverables` | Work that actually existed; omit unsupported channel lists |
| `media` | Asset, caption, alt text, source, placeholder status and credit information |
| `outcomes` | Optional evidence-backed result; omit if not supplied |
| `links` | Project-specific live/demo/source/campaign links, with actual destination checked |
| `publicationStatus` | Draft, preview, or ready; separates development placeholders from finished work |
| `factStatus` | Owner supplied, source inventory only, or needs clarification |

`deliveryContext = unresolved` is an internal content state. Public copy should use neutral project/contribution wording until a precise relationship is established. Do not publish “Direct client” by default.

### Software-specific fields

- Product purpose and users.
- Joshua's implemented areas and boundaries of responsibility.
- Technologies actually used in this project.
- Technical constraints, decisions and tradeoffs.
- Demonstration, screenshots or interaction recording.
- Outcomes only where there is evidence.

Recommended section labels: **Overview / My contribution / Technical decisions / Project views / Links & credits**. Add **Outcome** when supported. A case study can be useful without invented usage or performance metrics.

### Creative & marketing-specific fields

- Brand and actual campaign/brief.
- Audience and objective as supplied by the brief.
- Joshua's creative/marketing role and personal deliverables.
- Channels and formats actually worked on.
- Agency/employer delivery credit when applicable.
- Final campaign/creative assets and contextual captions.
- Results with their basis, time period and attribution limits when available.

Recommended section labels: **The brief / My role / Work delivered / Creative work / Credits**. Add **Results** only when substantiated. Do not turn a collection of graphics into a claim of campaign strategy, media buying or end-to-end delivery without confirming those responsibilities.

## Honest placeholder policy

During development, placeholders are acceptable and should be explicit:

| Situation | Label / behavior |
|---|---|
| Real campaign record, final visual not available | “Campaign image placeholder” on the preview media; use a neutral abstract composition without simulated branded work |
| Project details not yet recorded | Internal `[NEED: brief / role / deliverables]`; do not publish a fabricated narrative |
| Original speculative creative exercise | “Exploratory concept” and “Self-initiated work”; keep separate from the brand-experience roster |
| Neutral mockup used to stage the layout | “Presentation mockup” where it could be mistaken for final project work |
| Brand logo not supplied | Use the supplied brand name as text; do not invent a logo or subbrand |
| Outcome unknown | Omit the outcome section; describe the actual deliverable when known |

Do not place an invented campaign inside a real brand's card and rely on a small footer disclaimer. Keep placeholder status visible at the media itself in development. In a published portfolio, an empty case-study template should be hidden or presented as a clear preview rather than filled with generic accomplishment language.
