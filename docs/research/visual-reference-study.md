# Visual reference study: ambitious portfolios with a coherent point of view

Researched 29 October 2026. Prepared for an immersive and experimental rebuild that must serve prospective clients and recruiters equally. This is research and art-direction guidance, not a claim that the references pass accessibility, performance, or usability audits.

## Evidence and limits

- **V — visual observation:** current desktop page inspected through the in-app browser at approximately 1280 × 720. These are limited observations, not exhaustive interaction tests or mobile audits.
- **C — creator evidence:** the site owner's own writing, source code, or a case study explicitly authored by its creators. This supports implementation claims only for the stated project/version.
- **T — document evidence:** current first-party page content fetched and read. This verifies content, not the final rendered appearance.
- **I — analysis/inference:** our interpretation and proposed application. It is not a claim about the site's implementation or conversion results.

The browser environment adds extension controls, and one Dennis capture included an extension notice. Those overlays are not part of the designs. Native scrolling was unavailable in this subagent browser context. Navigation, screenshots and one semantic click into Unseen worked. Bruno was observed at its 3D entry scene; its full game was not played. Bisous's document loaded, but its image rendering could not be confirmed in the captured view. No frame-rate, network, Lighthouse, accessibility or device measurements were made.

## 1. Bruno Simon: build a world only this practitioner could have made

**Observed:** a miniature car sits inside a luminous violet environment, with clear directional lighting, a glowing ground boundary, foliage and hand-drawn entry lettering. The visitor is being invited into an experience rather than shown a standard hero. **Creator evidence:** the current site explains its Three.js/TSL rendering, WebGL/WebGPU support, Rapier physics and Howler audio; it links devlogs, source and Blender resources. The repository documents a staged game loop and asset compression pipeline. [Site](https://bruno-simon.com/), [source and production notes](https://github.com/brunosimon/folio-2025).

**I — why it works:** the portfolio is itself a large proof of the skill being sold. Scene design, controls, lighting, sound and physics belong to one idea. **Transfer:** author one interactive scene around the owner's actual discipline. **Do not copy:** the car, miniature world, palette or game rules. A physics game becomes a large continuing product; the relevant lesson is conceptual consistency, not making every portfolio a driving game. A direct route to work and contact would be essential in our client/recruiter brief.

## 2. Unseen Studio: atmosphere is a system

**Observed:** an understated pale-pink entry with a playful eye motif leads, through an explicit entry control, into a soft architectural scene: arched openings, steps, water, spherical forms and diffused light. Navigation remains quiet. An “enter without audio” choice is available. [Live site](https://unseen.co/).

**C:** in a studio-authored discussion, Unseen describes turning a typography launch into an interactive oasis and architecture work into a tactile 3D project ring. Those are distinct project concepts derived from the subject, not a claim that every Unseen project uses the homepage's exact technology. [Creator account](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/20/the-craft-behind-memorable-digital-experiences-inside-unseen-studio/).

**I — transfer:** define a small atmospheric vocabulary—material, light, camera behavior, typography and sound—and use it consistently. A custom object or spatial project navigator could express the owner's way of building software. **Risk:** beautiful entry sequences can delay evidence. Our version should give immediate work, about, résumé and contact paths, with sound optional and a fast entry route.

## 3. Lusion: spectacle has a clear stage

**Observed:** the current opening combines an off-white background, a concise large black statement, simple top-right controls and a large rounded viewport filled with glossy blue, white and black 3D forms. Text and 3D have distinct jobs. The header is readable while the image supplies most of the visual energy. [Live site](https://lusion.co/).

**T:** Lusion explicitly presents a multidisciplinary practice involving art direction, motion, WebGL, modeling and optimization. Its service list is evidence of the asset and production skills behind this category of work, not proof of a particular framework on every page. [Studio capabilities](https://lusion.co/about/).

**I — transfer:** a substantial 3D scene can coexist with direct orientation and uncomplicated navigation. The premium impression depends on authored geometry, materials, light and framing as much as animation. **Do not copy:** arbitrary floating chrome objects. Choose objects with a relationship to the portfolio. If the object has no explanatory or emotional purpose, a beautifully edited project film may be the stronger centerpiece.

## 4. Aristide Benoist: motion and curation as authorship

**Evidence:** the current site lists selected work with roles, clients and dates. Its owner explicitly specializes in motion and interaction; some project descriptions name native WebGL. The root agent's desktop screenshot showed a horizontal image strip against near-black, with restrained navigation. These facts do not establish the portfolio's complete rendering stack. [Portfolio](https://aristidebenoist.com/).

**I — transfer:** give project selection a designed rhythm. Unusual image sizes, strong crops, calibrated gaps, and continuous transitions can make browsing feel like an authored sequence. Keep project role, outcome and direct destination available. The design can be experimental without requiring every visitor to discover hidden navigation. **Do not copy:** exact image-strip behavior or decorative letter distortion simply because it looks technical. A distinctive composition should reflect the strongest work and number of projects actually available.

## 5. Bisous: rigorous composition behind cinematic transitions

**C:** creator Beaucoup describes a four-column backbone, a restrained palette, complementary sans and mono typography, a full-screen vertical project slider, direct work navigation and continuous transitions into the next project. Its disclosed stack is WordPress, GSAP and a custom Vimeo integration. [Creator case study](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/06/29/inside-bisous-designing-an-editorial-experience-for-cinematic-cgi/), [current site](https://bisous-production.com/).

**I — transfer:** let project imagery carry mood while a strict underlying grid connects very different layouts. A project-to-case-study transition can preserve an image's position or scale, then reveal the deeper story. That is a higher-value motion investment than animating every label. **Risk:** an infinite slider is not a universal navigation solution. Our portfolio should also expose stable project links, browser history, keyboard access and a touch-friendly index. The rendering of the live home could not be visually verified in this session, so this assessment uses the creator's account rather than claiming a complete live observation.

## 6. Dash: turn a concept into physical behavior

**C:** Dash explains how its “magnetic commerce” concept shaped the logo and cursor response. Its hero distorts video texture coordinates in a fragment shader; momentum follows pointer direction and decays. The creators disclose Webflow, GSAP and custom WebGL, and explicitly note that touch interaction should have been considered earlier. [Creator technical breakdown](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/21/magnetic-commerce-building-the-dash-creative-website/).

**I — transfer:** use one physical rule that belongs to our concept. Attraction, assembly, alignment, reveal or transmission could provide that rule, depending on the actual personal story. Apply it to a limited set of meaningful moments. **Do not copy:** a full-screen ripple just because it is available. Plan touch behavior while deciding the effect, rather than removing the effect afterward. This reference is particularly useful because it exposes how the visual idea became shader inputs and motion behavior. Its live rendering was not inspected here.

## 7. Studio Freight: identity and actual work provide substance

**Observed:** the current homepage places many image tiles around a central serif statement on a white field. Serif navigation, a small crest and tightly arranged thumbnails produce an unusual composition without relying on generic feature cards. **C:** the studio's own reintroduction explains a deliberate new identity and an illustration language derived from its principles and team. [Live site](https://studiofreight.com/), [identity case study](https://studiofreight.com/work/studio-freight).

**I — transfer:** strong art direction is an identity system plus material worth looking at. A spatial archive of project artifacts could show breadth while inviting exploration. The lesson is especially useful if we have several visually different real projects. **Risk:** a crowded field without strong assets looks arbitrary. Repeating filler thumbnails cannot replace finished work. Current 2026 branding also differs from many older screenshots circulating online; always identify the version being referenced.

## 8. Rauno Freiberg: the controls can demonstrate the craft

**Observed:** large white panels on a gray field use oversized black typography and a vivid yellow disc. The composition behaves like a collection of objects rather than a conventional section stack. **T:** the site links a large craft archive of interaction experiments, prototypes and essays. [Current site](https://rauno.me/), [craft archive](https://rauno.me/craft).

**I — transfer:** a portfolio can prove attention to detail in the small acts visitors perform: a project selector, media control, index expansion or transition into a case study. An interaction should remain satisfying after the novelty fades. **Do not copy:** component demos without explaining the owner's contribution. The work must answer a client or hiring question. Also avoid assuming that the current site matches its 2022 or 2023 archived editions; the owner explicitly links those editions separately.

## 9. Dennis Snellenberg: confidence in a few large decisions

**Observed:** a full-bleed gray portrait anchors the opening; a very large cropped moving name runs across it, quiet white navigation sits at the top, and a compact location marker and role statement balance the frame. The hierarchy feels intentional because the portrait, type and framing support one another. **T:** Dennis describes his focus on micro-animation, transitions and interaction, with Webflow or Kirby used in his practice. That does not prove the current portfolio's exact complete stack. [Site](https://www.dennissnellenberg.com/), [owner's service description](https://www.dennissnellenberg.com/about).

**I — transfer:** start with an uncompromising composition and high-quality material, then choreograph it. **Do not copy:** the now widely reproduced portrait-plus-marquee hero. It is recognizable authorship and easily becomes another template when borrowed. A personal portrait is only valuable if it fits our identity and is photographed/cropped to belong to the composition.

## What makes these experiences survive beyond an impressive short clip

These are design deductions from the study, not measured conversion findings.

1. **One conceptual world.** A visual rule links the opening, navigation, project sequence, case study and ending. It gives the experience a memory instead of a pile of effects.
2. **A complete journey.** Short reels often omit load time, touch behavior and the path to contact. Our prototype must include arrival → first project → case study → another project → résumé/contact → return navigation. Test this journey as carefully as the hero.
3. **Media with authorship.** Textures, geometry, lighting, project captures, editing and crops need production work. Substituting generic assets weakens even excellent code.
4. **A controlled rhythm.** A scene that demands attention is followed by readable stillness. A large image may be followed by small precise metadata, then a change in spatial composition. Constant maximum intensity flattens the experience.
5. **A useful second visit.** The visitor can go directly to work, skip an introduction, follow stable URLs, recover through back/forward, and see a clear role and outcome. A repeated cinematic entrance must not become a recurring obstacle.
6. **Touch as an authored experience.** Touch is not a cursor with less precision. Choose drag, tap, orientation or scroll behaviors that feel complete on a phone. Device permission should not be required for the normal experience.
7. **A deliberate fallback.** The underlying work and contact route remain legible without a GPU effect or long media load. Reduced motion can preserve the visual identity through composition, framing and instantaneous state changes.

## Three ambitious concept families to prototype

These are original working hypotheses. Select only after checking the owner's positioning and assets; they are not promises that the referenced sites use these exact patterns.

### A. Cinematic laboratory

An authored 3D artifact assembles, transforms or reveals its internal structure as the opening unfolds. Its behavior expresses the owner's actual work. Camera framing and typography establish the atmosphere, then the artifact becomes a recurring visual device between project chapters. The main navigation and first project remain immediately reachable.

- **Best fit:** a developer whose strongest story is turning complex systems into coherent experiences.
- **What it needs:** a genuinely specific artifact, modeling/material work, an intentional camera sequence, and a useful low-motion composition.
- **Prototype:** one short opening plus the transition into one real case study. Validate that the effect adds meaning and still runs acceptably on a representative phone.

### B. Spatial archive of work

Projects exist as objects, frames or artifacts within a designed field. Visitors can explore the field or use a simple persistent index. Selecting a project brings its artifact into a case study through a spatially continuous transition. The field's order, density or grouping should communicate something real about the work.

- **Best fit:** several strong projects with distinctive artifacts or screens.
- **What it needs:** carefully edited thumbnails, object framing, strong spatial rules, direct links and a deliberate touch layout.
- **Prototype:** three projects, one selection transition, one case study, an index, and browser-back behavior. Avoid building a vast explorable world before this path works.

### C. Continuous cinematic portfolio

A scroll-led sequence uses expressive typography, unusual image compositions, intentional color changes and image continuity across routes. A small shader or 3D element supplies a signature moment, while most of the experience is authored DOM, media and motion. Case studies feel like chapters in the same film, while navigation remains direct.

- **Best fit:** limited original 3D assets but strong interface work, screen recordings and a desire for ambitious motion.
- **What it needs:** image/video editing, strong type direction, transition choreography and pacing across different page lengths.
- **Prototype:** opening → project reveal → case study → next project. This can achieve substantial cinematic quality without making the entire website a game engine.

## Anti-generic rules for this rebuild

- Derive the signature from the owner's work or personal practice. Do not begin with a library's effect list.
- Do not use a dark background, glowing sphere, technical labels and marquee as a substitute for an idea.
- Avoid a standard stack of hero, three service cards, skill badges and generic testimonials dressed in a new palette.
- Keep unconventional layouts tied to reading order and a clear reason for each piece of content.
- Give work enough space and evidence. Project role, problem, decisions and outcome need specific honest content.
- Establish type, image treatment, palette proportions, spacing and motion behavior as one system before expanding pages.
- Prototype the hardest representative interaction with real content and touch behavior early.
- Use references as mechanisms to understand, not compositions to reproduce.
